# CrownPath — Non-Photo Scalp Observation Classroom Lab v0.1

**Curriculum design only · 2026-10-14 · No production or enrollment changes**

## Audience and scope

Learners in `barber-hair-scalp` and `cosmetology-hair-scalp`. Optional related camera lessons remain instructional only and do **not** require camera access. This lab is cosmetic education, not medical diagnosis or treatment.

## Learning outcomes

Learners can (1) describe visible hair/scalp features neutrally, (2) distinguish observation from diagnosis, (3) recognize when to pause a service and refer, (4) choose a conservative cosmetic care discussion, and (5) complete a lesson without uploading a photograph.

## Materials

Mannequin, clean comb and clips, task lighting, printed or instructor-created **synthetic** observation cards, paper worksheet. No participant images, real client names, or personal health histories.

## 25-minute guided lesson

| Time | Activity | Instructor prompt |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 min | Scope and consent | Explain that this is observation practice, not diagnosis; no photos are needed |
| 3–8 min | Observe a mannequin or synthetic card | Describe texture, density patterns, apparent flakes, oiliness, dryness, buildup or breakage without naming a disease |
| 8–13 min | Standardized comparison | Compare two synthetic cards under matched lighting and note uncertainty |
| 13–18 min | Safe decision exercise | Choose a gentle cosmetic service adjustment or pause/refer when concerning signs are presented |
| 18–23 min | Worksheet and peer review | Separate observations, unknowns, suggested cosmetic next steps, and referral reasons |
| 23–25 min | Exit quiz | Answer three safety-critical questions |

6. Was any real participant photo collected or uploaded? **NO** (required)

## Safety-critical exit questions (instructor answer key)

1. Can a photo or visual inspection establish exact scalp pH? **No.**
2. If a model has an open, bleeding area, should the learner proceed with a routine scalp service? **No; pause and follow appropriate referral protocols.**
3. May a learner name a medical disease based on visible flaking alone? **No; describe visible findings neutrally and refer when indicated.**

All three must be correct for the safety checkoff; allow remediation and reattempt. This is a **proposed** classroom exercise, not a claim that online grading or checkoff enforcement is implemented.
