# CrownPath 30-Day Pilot Validation Plan

**Status:** Owner preparation  
**First offer:** Scalp Care Foundations for barbers and cosmetologists  
**Pilot size:** 5–10 invited testers  
**Access:** Owner-controlled private testing  
**Purpose:** Prove that the course is clear, useful, safe, and valuable before expanding CrownPath or spending heavily.

## 1. Pilot outcome

At the end of 30 days, CrownPath will make one evidence-based decision:

- **Proceed:** The lesson works, testers understand it, safety checks pass, and there is credible customer interest.
- **Revise and retest:** The idea is useful, but the lesson, workflow, or offer needs correction.
- **Pause or pivot:** Testers do not identify enough value after meaningful revisions.

CrownPath will not be judged by the number of planned features. It will be judged by whether real people can use the first offer successfully.

## 2. Pilot boundaries

The pilot includes:

- Scalp Care Foundations lesson
- Manual scalp and hair observation exercise
- Dry-scalp and damaged-hair cosmetic care guidance
- Continue, modify, stop, or refer decision practice
- Safety knowledge check
- Short learner feedback form
- Owner issue and correction record

The pilot excludes:

- Automated image detection
- Diagnosis of disease
- Exact scalp pH estimates from photographs
- Prescribing treatment
- Surgical hair-implantation instruction
- Automatic certificates, rewards, or payments

A photograph is used for visible documentation and guided observation. The licensed professional or learner records what they can see. CrownPath does not claim that a photograph proves a medical condition.

## 3. Success standards

The pilot is ready to proceed when all of these standards are met:

1. At least 5 testers complete the selected lesson and practical exercise.
2. At least 80% of invited testers who begin the pilot finish it.
3. Average clarity and usefulness ratings are 4 out of 5 or higher.
4. Every tester passes the safety-critical questions after any required remediation.
5. At least 3 testers say they would use, recommend, or consider paying for the complete course.
6. No unresolved high-severity safety, privacy, access, or navigation issue remains.
7. The owner completes the final review and records a Proceed decision.

These are pilot decision standards, not promises of market success.

## 4. Four-week schedule

### Week 1 — Prepare

- Select one complete lesson for the pilot.
- Confirm the manual scalp-observation exercise.
- Check all links, buttons, instructions, and mobile layout.
- Confirm that camera consent and photo-use wording are visible.
- Prepare participant codes such as CP-01 through CP-10.
- Keep names, medical details, and unnecessary personal information out of the pilot record.

### Week 2 — Observe real use

- Invite 5–10 barbers, cosmetologists, or students.
- Ask each tester to complete the lesson without coaching first.
- Record where the tester pauses, becomes confused, or asks for help.
- Have each tester complete the manual observation and safety check.
- Collect the feedback form immediately after completion.

### Week 3 — Correct and retest

- Group issues by low, medium, or high severity.
- Correct confusing language, missing instructions, broken navigation, or safety concerns.
- Retest every high-severity correction.
- Ask at least two testers to repeat the revised parts.
- Preserve the original findings and record what changed.

### Week 4 — Decide

- Calculate completion, clarity, usefulness, and safety results.
- Review tester comments and willingness-to-pay responses.
- Confirm that high-severity issues are closed.
- Choose Proceed, Revise and Retest, or Pause/Pivot.
- Record the reason and the next 30-day action.

## 5. Tester flow

1. Read the pilot purpose and photo-consent notice.
2. Complete the selected Scalp Care Foundations lesson.
3. Review or take a client/scalp photo only with proper consent.
4. Record visible observations using neutral language.
5. Choose Continue, Modify, Stop, or Refer.
6. Select general cosmetic care guidance within professional scope.
7. Complete the safety knowledge check.
8. Submit the learner feedback form.

## 6. Manual observation categories

Testers may document visible signs such as:

- Flaking or surface dryness
- Oiliness or visible buildup
- Redness or irritation
- Hair breakage, split ends, or rough texture
- Signs of heat, chemical, or tension stress
- Areas that require the service to stop and a referral to a qualified medical professional

The form should use phrases such as “visible flaking observed” or “client reports tightness.” It should not label a disease or claim certainty from an image.

## 7. Learner feedback form

Use these questions after every test:

1. Was the lesson easy to understand? **1–5**
2. Was the manual observation process easy to follow? **1–5**
3. Did the service decision guidance feel clear? **1–5**
4. Did you understand when to stop and refer? **Yes / Partly / No**
5. What part helped you most?
6. What part confused you?
7. What information was missing?
8. Would you use this during training or client consultation? **Yes / Maybe / No**
9. Would you recommend the complete course? **Yes / Maybe / No**
10. What price range would feel reasonable for the complete course?
11. What is the single most important improvement CrownPath should make?

## 10. Spending rule

During the pilot, CrownPath should spend only what is needed to test the lesson safely. Do not use a line of credit or take on major software, marketing, inventory, or staffing commitments until the pilot results show real use and customer interest.
