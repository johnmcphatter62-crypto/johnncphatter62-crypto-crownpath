# CrownPath Home Care Pathway
## Full Lesson-by-Lesson Student Packet

**Publication layer:** Student / Learner-Safe

This packet expands the Home Care pathway into classroom-ready lesson pages. It intentionally excludes instructor answer keys, rationales, instructor-only notes, private records, and restricted operational information.

> **Scope notice:** CrownPath Home Care education is non-medical. It does not authorize diagnosis, treatment, medication administration, invasive procedures, emergency-service replacement, or tasks requiring a healthcare credential unless the learner is separately trained, authorized, and legally permitted. Employer procedures, lawful scope, emergency procedures, and current jurisdiction-specific requirements control when more specific.

# HC-1 — Client Safety & Home Care Foundations

**Estimated learning time:** 120 minutes

## Lesson Purpose
Build a safe, respectful foundation for non-medical home-care support centered on dignity, choice, environmental awareness, professional boundaries, escalation, and dependable routines.

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, the learner should be able to:
- identify basic environmental and client-safety priorities;
- protect dignity, privacy, choice, and personal boundaries;
- explain non-medical role limits;
- use a structured begin-task-check-finish routine;
- recognize when a concern requires supervisory, professional, or emergency escalation.

## Materials
- Home-safety checklist
- Client preference sheet
- Task plan
- Emergency/contact procedure
- Documentation form
- Hand-hygiene supplies

## Lesson 1 — Dignity and Client Choice
Home care occurs in the client's personal space. A professional should explain the task before beginning, ask permission, protect privacy, respect routines and preferences, and support the client's choices within the authorized service plan. Assistance should not become control.

# HC-2 — Sanitation & Infection Control

**Estimated learning time:** 105 minutes

## Lesson Purpose
Use practical infection-control and sanitation habits in non-medical home care, including hand hygiene, clean/used separation, PPE awareness, surface/equipment cleaning, linen handling, waste control, and exposure escalation.

## Learning Objectives
- Identify common contamination routes.
- Use hand hygiene at appropriate times.
- Apply clean-versus-used separation.
- Use PPE according to task and procedure.
- Clean reusable surfaces/equipment and handle waste safely.

## Lesson 1 — How Contamination Moves
Hands, shared surfaces, equipment, linens, body fluids, and improperly handled waste can transfer contaminants. Infection control reduces opportunities for transfer by interrupting these routes.

### Contamination Map

1. ______________________________________
2. ______________________________________

## Lesson 2 — Hand Hygiene
Clean hands before and after client-contact tasks, after glove removal, after contact with contaminated items, and at other times required by procedure. Follow the appropriate method for soap and water or approved hand sanitizer based on the situation.

## Lesson 3 — Gloves and PPE
Use gloves and other protection when required by the task or procedure. Gloves do not replace hand hygiene. Change or remove contaminated gloves before touching clean items and dispose of single-use PPE appropriately.

## Lesson 4 — Clean and Used Zones
Keep clean supplies away from used tools, contaminated surfaces, dirty linens, and waste. Avoid placing clean items on unknown or visibly soiled surfaces.

## Lesson 5 — Cleaning and Disinfection
Follow the product label and approved procedure, including dilution, compatibility, wet/contact time, ventilation, and safe storage. Do not invent or improvise chemical mixtures.

## Lesson 6 — Linens and Waste
Handle used linens with minimal shaking, separate them from clean items, and follow the approved laundry process. Dispose of waste according to the correct procedure for the setting.

## Lesson 7 — Exposure or Spill Response
When an exposure, spill, splash, or contamination event occurs, stop the task as needed and follow the approved response procedure. Seek supervisory, occupational, professional, or emergency help when required rather than improvising.

### CrownPath Required Progress Steps
1. Clean hands before and after client-contact tasks.
2. Use gloves or other protection when the task requires it.
3. Clean reusable work surfaces and equipment according to product directions.
4. Dispose of contaminated materials safely.

# HC-3 — Professional Communication

**Estimated learning time:** 90 minutes

## Lesson Purpose
Build calm, respectful, client-centered communication skills for introductions, active listening, boundaries, privacy, conflict reduction, change reporting, accessibility, and professional handoffs.

## Learning Objectives
- Use respectful client-centered language.
- Listen and confirm instructions instead of guessing.
- Protect private information.
- Report observations and changes clearly.
- Use professional escalation during conflict, uncertainty, or urgent concerns.

## Lesson 1 — Introduce and Explain
Identify yourself, explain why you are there, describe the authorized task, and confirm the client's readiness. Use plain language when it is clearer than jargon.

## Lesson 2 — Active Listening
Allow the client to finish, clarify what you heard, and ask focused questions when instructions are unclear. Repeat back important information when doing so helps prevent error.

## Lesson 3 — Respect and Boundaries
Use the client's preferred name and communication style when known. Avoid arguments, pressure, or promises about services/outcomes outside your authority.

## Lesson 4 — Privacy
Discuss private information only with authorized people and in appropriate settings. Avoid unnecessary details in public spaces, personal messaging accounts, or conversations with unrelated people.

## Lesson 5 — Accessibility and Communication Needs
Use available supports such as written instructions, larger text, captions, slower pacing, or approved interpretation resources when appropriate. Confirm understanding rather than assuming it.

## Lesson 6 — Reporting Changes
Use specific factual information: what was observed or reported, when it happened, what task was occurring, and what action was taken. Avoid diagnosis and speculation.

## Lesson 7 — Conflict and Urgent Escalation
Stay calm, prioritize immediate safety, avoid escalating arguments, and use supervisory or emergency channels when needed.

### CrownPath Required Progress Steps
1. Introduce yourself and explain the purpose of the interaction.
2. Listen without interrupting and confirm what you heard.
3. Keep private information limited to authorized people.
4. Use factual language when documenting or reporting.

## CLEAR Communication Reminder
- **C — CONNECT:** introduce yourself and explain purpose.
- **L — LISTEN:** let the client speak and confirm what you heard.
- **E — EXPLAIN:** use plain respectful language.
- **A — AUTHORIZED:** share private information only with approved people.
- **R — REPORT:** facts, time, action, and escalation—not diagnosis.

# HC-4 — Care Documentation

**Estimated learning time:** 90 minutes

## Lesson Purpose
Create clear, timely, factual home-care records that separate observation from opinion, protect confidentiality, document authorized services, correct errors through the approved process, and support safe handoffs.

## Learning Objectives
- Separate facts from opinions and diagnoses.
- Record authorized services accurately and promptly.
- Document meaningful observations and actions.
- Correct errors using the approved process.
- Protect confidential records.

## Lesson 1 — Why Documentation Matters
Documentation creates continuity. A useful record helps authorized people understand what service occurred, what was observed or reported, what action was taken, and whether someone was notified.

## Lesson 2 — Facts, Not Diagnoses
Record what you saw, heard, did, and were told. Avoid labels, assumptions, blame, unsupported conclusions, and medical diagnosis outside your role.

### Compare
**Opinion:** “The client was difficult.”

**Factual version:** __________________________________________
____________________________________________________________

## Lesson 3 — Core Record Elements
Where required by the approved record system, include:
- date and time;
- authorized service/task;
- relevant factual observation;
- client-reported information identified as such;
- action taken;
- notification/escalation;
- required name/signature/identifier.

## Lesson 4 — Timeliness
Complete records as required by the organization. Delayed memory can reduce accuracy. Never invent missing details.

## Lesson 5 — Corrections
Use the approved correction procedure. Do not secretly erase, rewrite, or falsify a record to hide an error.

## Lesson 6 — Confidentiality
Use authorized record systems. Do not place client information in personal notes apps, unapproved cloud storage, public messaging services, or other unauthorized locations.

# HC-5 — Wellness Client Experience & Professional Boundaries

## Lesson Purpose
Connect safe Home Care service with a respectful client journey while maintaining professional role boundaries.

## Client Journey
**BOOK / PREPARE → CONSULT / CONFIRM → SERVE → HANDOFF → RECOVER → FOLLOW UP**

### Prepare
Know the authorized service, schedule, approved contact process, accessibility needs that have been appropriately shared, and relevant safety procedures.

### Confirm
Explain the purpose of the visit and confirm the authorized task. Do not promise services outside the approved plan.

### Serve
Protect dignity, comfort, privacy, and choice. Stay within role and training.

### Handoff
Share only the minimum necessary information with authorized people for continuity and safety.

### Service Recovery
When a concern or complaint occurs:
1. listen without arguing;
2. document facts;
3. explain the approved process;
4. resolve only within your authority;
5. escalate when required;
6. record the outcome.

### Follow Up
Use approved communication channels and consent practices. Avoid unnecessary personal contact or communication outside professional boundaries.
