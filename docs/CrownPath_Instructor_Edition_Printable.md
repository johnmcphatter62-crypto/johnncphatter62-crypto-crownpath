# CrownPath Instructor Edition
## Printable Teaching, Assessment & Practical Manual

**Edition:** Instructor / Restricted

This manual supports authorized CrownPath instructors. It contains teaching guidance, assessment keys, practical scoring standards, remediation guidance, and instructor-only material that must not be exposed in learner-facing APIs, Student Edition exports, public bots, or learner avatars.

> **Regulatory notice:** CrownPath curriculum does not by itself establish licensure, continuing-education approval, State Board credit, medical authority, massage licensure, or permission to perform regulated services. Before representing training as satisfying an official requirement, verify the current requirement directly with the applicable official authority.

---

# Instructor Operating Standard

For every lesson, the instructor should:
1. Confirm the learner is in the correct pathway and has completed prerequisites.
2. State the lesson purpose, scope, and prohibited/out-of-scope activities.
3. Review sanitation, privacy, consent, equipment, product, and emergency controls applicable to the lesson.
4. Teach the concepts before demonstrating the practical sequence.
5. Demonstrate slowly enough for learners to observe critical control points.
6. Supervise guided practice and stop unsafe actions immediately.
7. Administer learner-facing knowledge checks without exposing the answer key beforehand.
8. Score practical performance using observable criteria.
9. Remediate failed safety-critical criteria before allowing progression.
10. Record completion according to CrownPath and any applicable official requirements.

## General Practical Rating

- **3 — Independent/Competent:** Performs the criterion safely and consistently without prompting.
- **2 — Developing:** Performs safely with minor prompting or correction.
- **1 — Needs Remediation:** Cannot yet perform consistently or requires substantial prompting.
- **0 — Stop / Unsafe:** Performs or attempts an unsafe, prohibited, deceptive, or out-of-scope action.

**Safety-critical rule:** A learner should not pass a practical solely on an average score when a safety-critical criterion is rated 0. Corrective instruction and repeat demonstration are required.

# PART I — HOME CARE INSTRUCTOR GUIDE

## HC-1 — Client Safety & Home Care Foundations

### Teaching Emphasis
Dignity, client choice, environmental awareness, authorized role limits, factual observation, and escalation. Repeatedly distinguish non-medical support from diagnosis, treatment, medication decisions, and clinical procedures.

### Demonstration
- Perform an environmental scan before touching supplies.
- Explain the task and obtain cooperation.
- Demonstrate hand hygiene and organized setup.
- Complete a simulated authorized task while checking comfort.
- Restore the environment and document a factual observation.

### Assessment Key
**Q: What should a learner do before starting an unfamiliar task?**
Correct: Confirm the approved plan, training, and instructions.

**Q: Which statement is factual rather than diagnostic?**
Correct example: “The client coughed repeatedly during the visit and reported feeling short of breath.”

### Practical Scoring
| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Environmental safety scan | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| Explains task / respects choice | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| Hand hygiene / preparation | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| Stays within authorized role | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| Recognizes escalation need | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| Documents facts without diagnosis | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |

### Remediation
Use short scenarios. Ask: **What did you observe? What are you authorized to do? Who must be notified? Is this an emergency?** Repeat until the learner can separate observation from diagnosis and routine reporting from emergency action.

---

## HC-2 — Sanitation & Infection Control

### Teaching Emphasis
Hands, clean/used separation, PPE, product-label compliance, reusable equipment, linen/waste handling, and exposure response. Never teach improvised chemical mixtures.

### Assessment Key
- Gloves replace hand hygiene: **No.**
- Cleaning/disinfection product use is controlled by: **Label directions and approved procedure.**

### Practical Safety-Critical Criteria
☐ Correct hand hygiene
☐ Prevents cross-contamination
☐ Uses/removes PPE appropriately
☐ Follows product label/contact time
☐ Does not invent chemical mixtures
☐ Knows exposure/spill escalation

### Remediation
Rebuild the workstation into clear **CLEAN** and **USED/CONTAMINATED** zones. Have the learner narrate every hand transition and explain why hand hygiene or glove change is needed.

---

## HC-3 — Professional Communication

### Teaching Emphasis
Introduction, active listening, repeat-back, privacy, accessibility, factual handoff, boundaries, conflict reduction, and urgent escalation.

### Assessment Key
- Unclear instruction: **Clarify and confirm before acting.**
- Best factual report: “At 2:10 PM the client reported dizziness after standing; I helped them sit safely and notified the supervisor.”

### Instructor Exercise
Give learners opinion statements such as “The client is difficult” and require conversion into observable facts, client quotes, time, action, and notification.

### Remediation
If a learner argues, guesses, or overpromises during role-play, reset the scenario and require the sequence: **Listen → Clarify → State boundary → Explain next authorized action → Escalate if needed.**

---

## HC-4 — Care Documentation

### Teaching Emphasis
Timeliness, factual language, client report versus observation, authorized service, actions, notifications, correction process, privacy, and secure storage.

### Assessment Key
- Appropriate note: “Client declined the scheduled walk at 10:00 AM and stated, ‘I feel tired’; supervisor notified per plan.”
- Client records belong: **Only in authorized systems or locations.**

### Practical Failure Conditions
Automatic remediation if the learner:
- knowingly creates false documentation;
- hides or deceptively alters an error;
- records a medical diagnosis outside role;
- places confidential records in an unauthorized system.

# PART II — BARBER INSTRUCTOR GUIDE

## BAR-1 — Barbering Foundations

### Teaching Emphasis
Station setup, sanitation, consultation, visible observation without diagnosis, tool selection, client protection, ergonomics, and closeout.

### Assessment Key
- Inspect tools: **Before use.**
- Scope-appropriate observation: “I can see visible flaking and redness, so we may need to modify or postpone service.”

### Demonstration Controls
Show clean/used zones, cord placement, blade inspection, guard selection, cape/neck protection, safe chair position, and complete station reset.

---

## BAR-2 — Hair & Scalp Science

### Teaching Emphasis
Hair structure, texture, density, growth direction, visible scalp observation, dryness, damage, conditioning, cosmetic pH concepts, product selection, and referral boundaries.

### Instructor Boundary
Never permit a learner or CrownPath AI to claim an exact scalp pH from a photograph. Camera/AI may support visible documentation but not diagnosis.

### Practical Prompt
Ask the learner to describe what is visible, choose a cosmetic service decision, and explain what would trigger referral. Correct diagnostic language immediately.

---

## BAR-3 — Scalp Camera & AI-Assisted Cosmetic Assessment

### Teaching Emphasis
Consent, privacy, standardized image capture, neutral visible categories, service planning, referral, and human responsibility.

### Safety-Critical Checkoff
☐ Permission before image capture
☐ Clean imaging equipment
☐ Consistent lighting/distance
☐ Neutral cosmetic observations only
☐ No diagnosis
☐ No exact pH claim from image
☐ Appropriate service/referral decision
☐ Protects image privacy

## BAR-4 — Cutting, Fading & Grooming

### Teaching Emphasis
Consultation-to-cut map, silhouette, face/head-shape considerations, growth pattern, guides, clipper/guard control, fade zones, shear/comb work, perimeter, facial-hair grooming where lawful, cross-checking, and finish.

### Assessment Key
- Purpose of a guide: **Provides a repeatable length reference.**
- Perimeter detailing: **After the major shape and blend are established.**
- Hot clipper blade: **Stop contact and follow safe cooling/maintenance procedures.**

### Face & Shape Coaching
Teach learners to use visible proportions, density, growth patterns, desired silhouette, and client preference to create balance. Avoid rigid claims that one face shape has only one correct haircut.

### Practical Scoring
| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Cutting plan / consultation | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| Establishes guide | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| Clipper/guard control | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| Builds transition zones methodically | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| Protects ears/skin | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| Cross-checks balance | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| Conservative perimeter/detailing | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |
| Sanitation/closeout | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ | ☐ |

### Remediation
Return to one isolated skill rather than repeating the entire haircut: guide creation, one transition zone, clipper-over-comb control, perimeter control, or cross-checking. Reintegrate only after the learner demonstrates the isolated skill safely.

---

## BAR-5 — Client Consultation & Shop Safety

### Assessment Key
- Damaged electrical equipment: **Remove from service and follow repair/replacement procedure.**
- Out-of-scope visible concern: **Describe neutrally, postpone/modify as appropriate, recommend evaluation.**
- Disinfectant contact time: **Follow label directions.**

### Stop Conditions
Unsafe electrical equipment, uncontrolled sharp-tool use, severe client discomfort, significant unexpected reaction, active bleeding requiring the applicable exposure response, or an out-of-scope condition requiring referral.

# PART III — COSMETOLOGY, BEAUTY & WELLNESS INSTRUCTOR GUIDE

## COS-1 — Cosmetology Foundations

### Teaching Emphasis
Professional preparation, consultation, visible observation, scope, service/product planning, client protection, closeout, and sanitation.

### Assessment Key
- Scope-appropriate response: “I can see visible redness and flaking, so I recommend postponing and seeking appropriate evaluation.”
- Product use: **Manufacturer directions and applicable rules.**

### Instructor Note
Require learners to distinguish cosmetic observation from diagnosis in both spoken consultation and written notes.

---

## COS-2 — Hair & Scalp Science

### Teaching Emphasis
Hair structure, visible scalp condition, porosity/elasticity concepts, dryness, damage, conditioning, cosmetic pH education, product selection, and referral.

### Key Boundary
CrownPath may teach pH concepts and appropriate measurement methods. **A photograph alone cannot establish an exact scalp pH.**

### Remediation
If a learner jumps directly from an image to a disease label or exact pH claim, require them to rewrite the assessment using only visible observations and a cosmetic proceed/modify/refer decision.

## COS-4 — Chemical Services & Product Safety

### Teaching Emphasis
Consultation, exact manufacturer instructions, required preliminary testing, PPE, ventilation, measurement/mixing, timing, monitoring, adverse reaction response, cleanup, disposal, and documentation.

### Assessment Key
- Mixing ratio: **Manufacturer directions for the exact product system.**
- Passed preliminary test guarantees no reaction: **No.**
- Serious unexpected burning: **Stop and follow the product's immediate-response directions; escalate care when indicated.**

### Automatic Stop / Remediation
- Inventing a chemical ratio.
- Improvised mixing contrary to manufacturer directions.
- Intentionally exceeding processing limits.
- Ignoring a significant adverse reaction.
- Continuing after an instructor safety stop.

### Instructor Demonstration Rule
Use only instructor-approved products and the exact instructions for that product. Do not turn general CrownPath examples into universal formulas.

---

## COS-5 — Non-Surgical Hair Replacement

### Teaching Emphasis
Cosmetic versus surgical distinction, consultation, visible scalp/hair observation, system categories, lawful attachment, fitting/blending, maintenance/removal, documentation, and referral.

### Assessment Key
- Outside CrownPath course: **Implanting harvested follicles through skin incisions.**
- Camera AI diagnoses cause of hair loss: **No.**
- Attachment removal: **Manufacturer directions, lawful scope, and approved removal products.**

### Prohibited Practical Content
No incision, graft harvesting, graft implantation, anesthesia, surgical transplantation, medication recommendation, or medical diagnosis.

---

## COS-6 — Professional Makeup Artistry

### Teaching Emphasis
Consultation, sanitation, hygienic dispensing, skin observation within cosmetic scope, complexion preparation, balance/color, eyes/lips, finishing, removal, and client education.

### Safety-Critical Criteria
☐ Clean/sanitized tools
☐ Hygienic dispensing/no unsafe double dipping
☐ Sensitivity/history consultation
☐ Does not apply over visibly unsafe/infectious/injured condition
☐ Protects eye/lip hygiene
☐ Explains safe removal

### Remediation
Use a mannequin or face chart to isolate color placement, balance, blending, and sequencing. Hygiene failures require a complete reset and repeat of sanitation/dispensing procedures.

## COS-7 — Manicure & Pedicure Nail Care

### Teaching Emphasis
Consultation, visible nail/skin observation, sanitation, shaping, cosmetic care, product directions, living-tissue boundaries, cleanup, and referral.

### Instructor Boundary
Do not permit learners to diagnose or treat nail disease. Postpone/refer when visible conditions appear unsafe or outside scope.

### Safety-Critical Criteria
☐ Sanitized station/implements
☐ Visible inspection
☐ No unauthorized cutting of living tissue
☐ Manufacturer-compliant product use
☐ Correct single-use disposal
☐ Reusable implement processing
☐ Appropriate referral decision

---

## COS-8 — Wellness Massage Foundations

### Teaching Emphasis
Non-medical scope, consent, privacy, draping, positioning, pressure communication, comfort, contraindication awareness, sanitation, and referral.

### Instructor Regulatory Rule
Massage scope and licensing vary. Do not represent this unit as authorization to practice massage therapy where a separate license or credential is required.

### Automatic Stop
Lack of consent, inappropriate draping/privacy, significant pain, concerning symptoms, prohibited technique, or activity outside the instructor/learner's lawful credential.

---

## COS-9 — Fitness, Recovery & General Wellness

### Teaching Emphasis
General warm-up, controlled movement, gradual progression, recovery, hydration, sleep, body awareness, and warning signs.

### Boundary
No individualized medical rehabilitation, diagnosis, medical nutrition therapy, or exercise prescription beyond the instructor's lawful qualifications.

### Instructor Exercise
Give learners scenarios involving ordinary exertion versus concerning symptoms. Require them to identify when to stop and seek qualified or emergency care rather than pushing through.

# PART V — AVATAR & BOT BUILDER INSTRUCTOR GUIDE

## Teaching Emphasis
Narrow role definition, approved knowledge, least privilege, learner/instructor separation, privacy, accessibility, prohibited actions, escalation, adversarial testing, human approval, audit, and versioning.

### Assessment Key
- What grants protected-record permission? **Server-side authorization and approved capabilities.**
- Learner bot asked for instructor answers: **Refuse restricted material and continue with permitted learning help.**
- Unverified regulatory information: **Mark uncertainty and route for qualified human verification.**

### Required Red-Team Tests
Every learner bot design should be tested against:
1. a normal course question;
2. an ambiguous question;
3. a request for the answer key;
4. a medical diagnosis request;
5. a permission-bypass request;
6. a privacy-sensitive request;
7. an out-of-scope request;
8. a request to override Owner/security controls.

### Automatic Failure Conditions
- Bot is given instructor answer keys for learner use.
- Bot can grant itself permissions.
- Bot is configured to diagnose or prescribe.
- Bot can approve regulated credentials without authorized human control.
- Bot can override Owner/security controls.
- Bot publishes or sends restricted/private information without authorization.
